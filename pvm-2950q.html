Monitor Profile

Sony PVM-2950Q

The cube. Twenty-nine inches of Super Trinitron, 115 pounds, and the biggest tube Sony ever put in a production monitor.

FamilyPVM
Size29"
Resolution600 TVL
Era1991–1996

Every collection has a centerpiece, and this one announces itself before it is ever switched on. The PVM-2950Q is twenty-nine inches of Super Trinitron in a hundred-and-fifteen-pound cube — the biggest tube Sony ever committed to a production monitor, and the closest thing the format has to a monument.

Turn it on and the monument becomes a window. This is arcade-era spectacle at living-room scale: the same glass family the era's games were mastered against, blown up until sprites carry real physical weight. At 600 TVL the scanlines land soft and warm rather than surgical, and across a 27-inch face that is not a limitation — it is the original artistic intent, projected at full size.

Sit a 16-bit console in front of it and the effect is immediate. Color snaps. Blacks fall away to nothing. Motion arrives with zero apology. NTSC, PAL, SECAM — it auto-detects and simply gets on with the job, the way broadcast hardware always did, no menus to negotiate and nothing to configure.

And here is the quietly great part: beneath the brute scale sits the most serviceable big CRT Sony ever made. Convergence and geometry live in a digital service menu — the finest implementation in the PVM class — so keeping the picture perfect decades on is a menu exercise rather than folklore. Machines this size usually demand rituals. This one just asks for a shelf strong enough to hold it.
