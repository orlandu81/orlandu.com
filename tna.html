Machine Profile

Total Nuclear Annihilation

Scott Danesi's synthwave fever dream. Fast, brutal, single-level, perfect.

Total Nuclear Annihilation should not work. It has one level. There is no mode select, no ramp-fed video mode, no licensed film to lean on. You are trying to blow up a reactor, then another reactor, then another, faster each time, until you fail. That is the entire game.

It works because Scott Danesi built it as a musician first. The soundtrack drives the machine — the tempo climbs with the reactor count, the lights lock to the beat, and by reactor four you are playing a rhythm game that happens to involve a steel ball. Spooky Pinball put it out in 2017 and it immediately embarrassed a lot of bigger, more expensive machines.

Why it belongs here

Every other machine in this row is licensed — a film, a show, a franchise. TNA is the one that had to earn attention on design alone, from a small manufacturer in Wisconsin, and did. In a room full of nostalgia it is the machine that is purely about how it plays.
