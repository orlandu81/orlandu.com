What is modeled, and what is not

Scan lines are placed 240 to the viewable height, so the gap between them is Sony’s picture height divided by 240 — 1.21 mm on a 20-inch tube, 1.71 mm on the 2950Q. Horizontal softness comes from the center-resolution spec: TV lines over a width equal to the picture height, so 1000 TVL on the BVM-20E1U resolves a 0.29 mm element where the 2950Q’s 600 TVL resolves 0.69 mm. The aperture grille is drawn at the pitch Sony states where it states one — 0.25 mm on the E and F tubes from the E/F manual, 0.7–0.85 mm on the 2950Q from its brochure; the PVM-20L5 has no published figure and is drawn at the 0.25 mm class its HR tube belongs to. The LCDs are drawn as their actual pixel grids — 640×480 on the 9020 and 9030, 1024×768 on the 9050, 800×480 on the 940W — with 240p scaled by nearest neighbor, which is an assumption about Sony’s scaler, not a fact; the 9050’s 3.2× fractional scale is why its text shows uneven pixel widths. Beam bloom, gamma, the damper wires and the eye’s one-arcminute limit are approximations. Nothing here is a photograph of these monitors; the gallery has those.
