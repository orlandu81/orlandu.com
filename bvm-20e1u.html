Monitor Profile

Sony BVM-20E1U

The grail. One thousand TV lines at twenty inches in 4:3 — a number Sony hit once, on this chassis, and never again.

FamilyBVM
Size20"
Resolution1000 TVL
Era1995–2002

Every collector has one machine that justifies the whole pursuit. This is that machine. One thousand TVL, at twenty inches, in 4:3 — a number Sony hit exactly once, on exactly this chassis, and never again. A grail earns the name on a single specification.

Inside is the M49LCB22X, an aperture grille so fine — roughly a quarter of a millimeter — that even the tube in its celebrated successor is coarser. This is the sharpest large-format 4:3 glass ever to leave the company, built in an era when a master control room would clear its budget for the reference seat. Today that reference seat belongs to something better: games.

Because here is the beautiful part — it is a standard-definition instrument by design. 240p and 480i are not formats it tolerates; they are what it is for. Feed it a Saturn or a Neo Geo and the scanline definition is as sharp as the format has ever been rendered, anywhere, by anything. This is the definitive large-format 240p display, and there is no asterisk on that sentence.

It is also a machine you get to build. Four option slots wait behind the fixed RGB block, and the full E/F card family runs to eight — a whole deck of possibilities. Composite is covered; the S-Video path is an open hunt running through a BKM-24N or 27T, tracked across two continents through auction alerts and a contact in Yamagata. Half the joy of a flagship is finishing it.

Sightings are rare enough to count as events. A sound example surfaces perhaps once a season, then vanishes into someone's climate-controlled bay — which is what grail tier actually means: not extravagance, but patience. This one's patience traces to October 1998 build stock, still holding a picture with no living rival.

Strictly SD forever. That is not the compromise — it is the specification.
