Sony Model Numbers Decoded:
What the Letters and Digits on a Badge Mean

Sony never published a key to its monitor model numbers. There is no Sony document, in any language, that says “E means this, 5 means that.” What there is instead is thirty years of manuals, brochures and press releases in which the same letters keep landing in the same places — and once you line them up, most of a Sony badge reads like a spec sheet. This page does that lining-up from Sony’s own literature, and says plainly where a reading is Sony’s and where it is a pattern. It sits beside the BKM card reference and PVM vs. BVM: three pages on what the badge tells you.

There is no official decoder

Sony’s manuals treat the model number as an opaque label. “The model and serial numbers are located at the rear. Record these numbers…” is all the PVM-20L5 operating instructions say about it5, and the LMD-9050/9030/9020 manual opens with the same sentence24. A 2012 thread asking how to read Sony model numbers got the consumer-set prefixes and screen sizes and nothing else; nobody could decode the pro lines12. The one collector attempt at a key cites no Sony source13.

So the honest frame: the prefix, the size and the region suffix are Sony-documented; the series letter and the grade digit are readable from Sony’s model lists but never defined by Sony; everything else is inference and is labeled as such. The strips below carry a tag per generation so a reader always knows which is which.

The line: PVM, BVM, LMD, BKM, AMS

PrefixWhat it isSource
PVMSony’s production monitor. Every PVM files under “Professional Monitors” in Sony’s catalogs, and the CCTV catalog carried the security tier. “Professional Video Monitor” is how collectors and this site expand it; no Sony brochure opened spells the initials out.1,4,8
BVMThe master and reference line — “a full and consistent line of master-grade CRT monitors” in the BVM-A brochure; the D brochure splits it into master monitors (32/24/20) and picture monitors (14/9). “Broadcast Video Monitor” is the collector expansion; Sony’s heading is “Broadcast and Professional Monitors.”1,2
LMDThe professional LCD line, branded LUMA in Sony’s own press material. Sony does not expand “LMD.”9,14
BKMThe prefix on every accessory for BVMs and late PVMs — option boards, control units, probes, memory cards, rack kits. It is an accessory family, not a card prefix; the cards themselves are on the BKM reference.1,2,3
AMSThe audio monitor speaker — the plate reads “Audio Monitor Speaker,” per the monograph, which has its own rulings and is not repeated here.15

Size: the number is the CRT, not the picture

Sony’s footnote on every brochure: “32”, 24”, 20”, 14” and 9” refer to the CRT size. For viewable area, measured diagonally, please refer to the specifications.”1 A 20L5 is a 19-inch picture; a 14F5U is 13⅛ inches; the 29-inch PVM-2950Q shows 271,2,7. Two-digit sizes are the rule from the D series on; the four-digit PVMs (1354, 1944, 2950, 8045) fold the size into the leading digits and use the rest as the model within that size — a pattern across every four-digit PVM in Sony’s catalogs4,10,11. The LMD line does the same with a trailing zero: 9050, 9020, 9030 are 9-inch; 2030W, 2050W, 2450W are 20 and 249,14. Drawn to Sony’s dimensions, the whole fleet is on the monitors page.

Sony-documented

Reading the PVM digit: within a series the digit is the tier — M2 below M4, L1 below L2 below L5 — and Sony’s brochures state the tube difference each time5,6,16,17. It is not a year and not a revision. Two more PVM markers: the Japan-market L5 carried a leading D (PVM-D20L5J), and a second D before the J marked a built-in SD/HD-SDI decoder (PVM-D20L5DJ)18; MD on an M2 or L2 is the medical variant of the same chassis, which Olympus also rebadged19.

Sony-documented

The E/F/G/A letters sit in two places and mean two things. Before the size they are the generation: D, then A. After the size they are the tube, and Sony’s E/F manual prints the table that defines it3:

ModelAperture grille pitchCenter resolution
BVM-14E1 / 14E50.22 mm900 TV lines
BVM-14F1 / 14F50.25 mm800 TV lines
BVM-20E10.25 mm1000 TV lines
BVM-20F10.30 mm900 TV lines

So E is the finer tube and F the step down: at the same size, E beats F by a hundred lines3. The D and A generations keep the letters — D20F1 at 0.30 mm and 900 lines, D24E1W at 0.25 mm and 1000, A20F1U at 0.30 mm1,2. H is the picture-monitor tube on the D9 and D14: 0.25 mm, 800 lines at 14 inches, 450 at 9, and the 9-inch is the only BVM tube with P-22 rather than SMPTE-C phosphors1. The G generation sits between F and D and is controlled from the same BKM-10R system1,3; its tube spec is in no document opened for this page.

The digit after the tube letter is Sony-stated, three times over. The D brochure: “Display unit (BVM-D14H1U/D9H1U) and stand-alone (BVM-D14H5U/D9H5U) monitors”1. The E/F manual: the 20E1/20F1/14E1/14F1 “are controlled by a separate control unit, such as a BKM-10R Monitor Control Unit,” while the 14E5/14F5 have the panel built in3. Sony Japan’s D-series release calls the D14H1J a display unit and the D14H5J an integrated-control model21; the BVM-A spec table says “Display unit” for the A20F1U and “Stand-alone monitor” for the A14F5U2. 1 = display unit, needs a BKM-10R, 11R or 15R. 5 = stand-alone, controls built in. Every 20-inch and larger BVM is a 1; the 14s and 9s came both ways. The practical consequence is on the BVM-20E1U profile: without its BKM-10R there is no way to adjust the monitor.

Earlier BVMs — the 1911, 2010, 2011P — used the four-digit PVM-style numbering; the tube-letter system starts with the E/F generation22.

Quick answers

What do the letters in a Sony PVM model number mean?

The prefix is the line (PVM production, BVM broadcast and master, LMD LCD), the number is the CRT size, the letter is the series — Q four-standard, M and L production tiers, N security — the digit is the grade within the series (M2 600 lines, M4 800; L1, L2, L5), and the last letter is the market (U USA, E Europe, J Japan). Sony never published a key; this is what its manuals show.

What is the difference between a BVM-D14H1U and a BVM-D14H5U?

Controls. Sony calls the 1 a “display unit” — it needs a BKM-10R control unit — and the 5 a “stand-alone monitor” with the control panel built in. The tube and inputs are the same.

What does the U or E at the end mean?

The market it was sold into: U is USA/Canada, E is Europe (CE-marked), J is Japan. On BVMs it also sets the phosphor: U units are SMPTE-C, E and J units are EBU.

What does Q mean on a PVM?

Every Q model displays four color systems — NTSC, PAL, SECAM and NTSC 4.43 — and Sony’s manuals say so in those words. QM is the export version of the same monitor.

Is a BVM “E” better than an “F”?

At the same size, yes: Sony’s own table gives the 14E a 0.22 mm grille and 900 TV lines against the 14F’s 0.25 mm and 800; the 20E1 reaches 1000 lines where the 20F1 reaches 900.
